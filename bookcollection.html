Over my high school years and my college years, I dabbled in some physics and mathematics. Did physics olympiad in high school, had many hyperfixations in college, so I'll give some resources that I've used in the past or even am currently reading. Note that the books I give a (*) means it's a more advanced book from the rest

Physics

Classical Mechanics

  1. Taylor’s Classical Mechanics (good explanations)
  2. Morin’s Introduction to Classical Mechanics (good problems)
  3. Greenwood’s Advanced Dynamics* (a bit more advanced, I use it for the hard problems)
  4. Goldstein’s Classical Mechanics* (bit more advanced too, more analytical mechanics and whatnot)

Electricity and Magnetism (EnM)

  1. Griffiths’ Introduction to Electrodynamics (good explanations, good problems, overall the best book for the area)
  2. Purcell’s Electricity and Magnetism (same level as Griffiths, I prefer Griffiths’ style more)
  3. Zangwill’s Modern Electrodynamics* (a bit more advanced, basically a good Jackson)
  4. Landau’s Electrodynamics of Continuous Media* (terse, evil, but kind of accessible; problems are hell)

Quantum Mechanics

  1. Griffiths’ Introduction to Quantum Mechanics (I don’t really like it but people seem to)
  2. Shankar’s Principles of Quantum Mechanics* (hits the right spots; people say it’s graduate level but honestly I think it's somewhere at the transition level; introduces math concepts in chapter 1 which is a plus)
  3. Sakurai’s Modern Quantum Mechanics* (roughly the same level as Shankar, a bit above maybe. it's pretty clear and good for me)

Thermodynamics / Statistical Mechanics

  1. Blundell’s Concepts in Thermal Physics (used this for olympiad; good book albeit less popular in the US apparently)
  2. Schroeder’s Thermal Physics (people like it but I haven’t read much; on first glance it looks good)
  3. Sears & Salinger’s Thermodynamics, Kinetic Theory, and Statistical Thermodynamics (I have a copy, I don’t really like it that much)
  4. Landau’s Statistical Physics* (haven’t read much but first few chapters seem good)

Condensed Matter Physics (CMP)

  1. Ashcroft & Mermin’s Solid State Physics (probably my favorite book on the topic; explanations are nice, extensive)
  2. Simons’ Introduction to Solid State Physics (good intro to the subject, mostly used by undergraduates)
  3. Kittel’s Introduction to Solid State Physics (I don’t really like it to be honest, just a style preference I think)

Mathematics

Real Analysis

  1. Abbott’s Understanding Analysis (nice first book for real analysis, doesn’t go too hardcore, explains things well)
  2. Rosenlicht’s Introduction to Analysis (used for MATH 424, good book and very concise; goes into metric spaces)
  3. Loeb’s Real Analysis* (currently going through it right now! I think it's okay so far)

Linear Algebra

  1. Axler’s Linear Algebra Done Right (people like it, I also find it quite good)
  2. Curtis’ Linear Algebra: an Introductory Approach (personally I think it's okay, my copy seems a bit old)

Complex Analysis

  1. Ahlfors’ Complex Analysis* (a very old book, the book is very tiny yet very extensive, which makes it scary)
  2. Nicolas’ notes for MATH 448 which is based on Palka's An Introduction to Complex Function Theory (implies that Palka is a good book)

Number Theory

  1. Strayer’s Number Theory (good book, presents things clearly)

Proofs

  1. Donaldson and Pantano’s An Introduction to Abstract Mathematics (good book; used this for MATH 347, very clear on how they do things)
  2. West’s Introduction to Mathematical Thinking* (very extensive; didn’t use it much but the amount of topics covered is quite astonishing)

Abstract Algebra

  1. Herstein’s Abstract Algebra (cool book, many exercises; overall I like this book)
  2. Lang's Algebra* (just skimmed a few chapters, this book is a beast)
